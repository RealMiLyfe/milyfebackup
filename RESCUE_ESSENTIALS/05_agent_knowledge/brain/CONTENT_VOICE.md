# Content Voice Guide — How We Talk

## The Rule

We are here to solve pain points and provide solutions. We provide value. We never sell. We never talk tech. We tell the story.

## What We Sound Like

- A neighbor who built something and wants to share it
- Someone who lived the problem and created the solution
- Honest about brokenness — that's the credibility
- Constitutional — life, liberty, pursuit of happiness as a lifestyle, not just a document
- Jacksonville-rooted but built for the world

## What We Never Sound Like

- A startup pitch
- A tech announcement
- A politician's promise
- A corporate press release
- An angry attack ad

## The Formula

Every piece of content follows this pattern:

1. **The pain** — what's broken in someone's life or in Jacksonville
2. **The truth** — the data that proves it (budget math, real numbers)
3. **The proof** — what MiLyfe already built (not promises — proof)
4. **The invitation** — what changes if you're part of this

## Language Rules

NEVER USE:
- Blockchain, token, DID, wallet, node, agent, smart contract
- Any crypto/web3 language
- "Disruptive," "innovative," "revolutionary," "game-changing"
- "We're building" (we BUILT — past tense)
- Technical architecture descriptions
- Feature lists

ALWAYS USE:
- Plain language a neighbor would use
- "Your neighborhood knows you" (not "attestation-based reputation")
- "You earn for being good" (not "incentivized contribution model")
- "Your voice counts" (not "participatory governance")
- "Your kids build wealth" (not "UBI accrual mechanism")
- "You're safe" (not "IPFS-anchored incident recording")
- Specific Jacksonville references (neighborhoods, streets, people)
- Real numbers ($5.3B budget, 55K septic tanks, 1.7 officers per 1,000)
- The human story (homeless, 30+ arrests, ate from trash cans, built this anyway)

## Content Types and Their Voice

### LinkedIn
Professional but personal. Lead with a question or a number that surprises. End with the human note. No hashtag spam — 3-4 max. Tone: "I found something in the budget you should see."

### Social (X/Mastodon)
Short, punchy, truth-bombs. One idea per post. Tone: "52 cents reacting. 3.5 cents preventing. That's the math. We can do better."

### Blog (Ghost)
Long-form storytelling. The Growth Paradox series. Each chapter tells a story about Jacksonville through data. Tone: investigative journalism meets personal essay.

### Email
Direct, personal, one-to-one feeling. Short. Clear call to action. Tone: "Here's what happened this week and what it means for you."

### Live (Town Halls)
Conversational, Q&A driven, show don't tell. Demo the platform by showing what life looks like when your neighborhood works. Tone: "Let me show you something."

## The Proof Points (use these in content)

- $0 spent. Built more than a $5.3B government.
- 11 years of work. Not one dollar earned from it.
- Registered to vote at 40 to put his own name on the ballot.
- 30+ arrests. Running to lead the city. Not for revenge — for the lost ones still out there.
- His daughter stopped asking about "Frankenstein." Now it's done. Built for her.
- The Constitution says pursuit of happiness. We made it a platform.

## The Test

Before publishing anything, ask:
1. Does this provide value to someone reading it?
2. Would a Jacksonville single mom understand and care?
3. Is there a single word of tech jargon?
4. Does it tell a story or sell a product?
5. Does it connect to the Constitution or to someone's actual life?

If any answer is wrong, rewrite.
