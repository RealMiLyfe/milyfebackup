# Jacksonville Takes In More Money Every Year. So Why Does Everything Feel Worse?

I've spent the last several weeks doing something nobody running for office usually does. I read the actual budget. Not the press release. Not the summary. The budget.

Here's what I found.

## Before Jacksonville Spends A Single Dollar On:

- Filling a pothole
- Hiring a new officer
- Connecting a septic tank to the sewer
- Building a park in a new neighborhood

Nearly 70 cents of every dollar is already committed.

## The Numbers

The pension crisis: $2.9 to $3.2 billion unfunded. Every year the city pays $280 to $320 million just to service that legacy debt. That money produces zero current-year services. It's paying for promises made decades ago.

The JEA transfer: roughly $115 million per year comes from your utility bill into the general fund. Did you know that? Your electric bill is subsidizing city operations. Nobody talks about it.

## How We Compare

Per capita spending across peer cities:

- Nashville: about $2,100 per resident
- Charlotte: about $1,950 per resident
- Austin: about $2,400 per resident
- Jacksonville: about $1,700 per resident

And a higher percentage of Jacksonville's $1,700 goes to legacy costs that produce nothing today. Less money per person. More of it pre-committed. That's the math.

## More Revenue Coming In. Measurably Worse Services Per Resident.

Jacksonville's revenue grows every year. Population grows. Tax base grows. But services per resident are declining because the structural commitments eat the gains before they reach your street.

That's not a headline. That's arithmetic.

## What I Did About It

I built the alternative budget model — the one that actually measures prevention versus reaction, that tracks where every dollar goes in real time, that shows you what your taxes produce.

I built it for $0. Using free tools. Running on one computer.

If I can do that with nothing, imagine what we do with the actual budget and actual accountability.
